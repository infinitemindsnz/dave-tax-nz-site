Three News aired a segment on 30 August 2026 covering the National Party's newly announced student loan policy, described by the party as the "Back Pocket Boost for Graduates". According to the broadcast, the proposal outlines a two-tiered approach to the Student Loan Scheme that would apply if National is re-elected.

The first element of the policy, as reported, is aimed at encouraging graduates to remain in New Zealand after completing their studies, framed as an incentive-based measure. The second element takes a firmer position on borrowers who move overseas and fall into default on their repayments, signalling a stricter enforcement stance than currently applies.

In the segment, which ran from the 0:43 to 4:09 mark of the bulletin and is available to view on ThreeNow, tax barrister Dave Ananth was featured as an industry commentator. Ananth specialises in overseas student loan debt matters at Meridian Partners. His analysis, as reported by Three News, acknowledged the underlying policy rationale: student loan debt is funded by taxpayers and must ultimately be repaid by borrowers.

However, Ananth cautioned that increasing penalties specifically targeted at overseas-based borrowers carries a risk of being counterproductive. His view, as conveyed in the broadcast, was that harsher penalties could push already disengaged borrowers further away from making contact with authorities or arranging repayment, rather than encouraging compliance.

The coverage positions the policy as part of a broader pre-election conversation about how the Student Loan Scheme should balance fairness to taxpayers against practical outcomes for borrowers living abroad. No further policy detail beyond the two-tiered structure and the overseas default measures was outlined in the segment as reported.
