## National's Proposed Student Loan Reform Gets Media Attention

Three News aired a segment on 30 August 2026 covering the National Party's newly announced policy proposal for the Student Loan Scheme, dubbed the "Back Pocket Boost for Graduates". According to the bulletin, the policy would introduce structural changes to how student loans are managed if National is re-elected.

The segment, which ran between 0:43 and 4:09 of the broadcast, described the proposal as a two-tiered strategy. On one hand, it is designed to incentivise graduates to remain in New Zealand after completing their studies. On the other, it takes a notably stricter approach toward borrowers who move overseas and fall behind on repayments.

## Industry Commentary from Dave Ananth

As part of the coverage, Dave Ananth, a tax barrister and specialist in overseas student loan debt at Meridian Partners, was interviewed to provide industry analysis on the proposal.

According to the report, Ananth acknowledged the underlying policy rationale: student loan debt is funded by taxpayers, and there is a legitimate expectation that it will ultimately be repaid. However, he raised concerns about the practical effect of increasing penalties specifically targeted at borrowers living overseas.

His central point, as reported by Three News, was that harsher penalties for overseas-based borrowers risk being counterproductive. Rather than encouraging repayment, tougher sanctions could push already disengaged borrowers further away from contact with Inland Revenue and the loan scheme altogether. In other words, a punitive approach may worsen the very compliance problem it is intended to solve.

## Why This Matters for Borrowers and Policy Watchers

The proposal, as covered in the segment, sits within a broader conversation about how New Zealand manages student loan debt held by graduates who relocate abroad. Overseas-based borrowers have long been a focus of IRD's compliance efforts, given the practical difficulties of enforcing repayment obligations across borders.

The "Back Pocket Boost for Graduates" framing suggests National intends to pair a domestic incentive — encouraging graduates to stay and work in New Zealand — with a firmer stance on those who leave and fall into arrears. The Three News segment did not detail the specific mechanics of either the incentive for domestic graduates or the exact nature of the stricter overseas measures beyond describing the general two-tiered structure.

Ananth's commentary, as reported, adds a practical industry perspective to the political announcement: policy settings intended to improve recovery of taxpayer-funded debt need to be weighed against the risk of borrowers disengaging entirely if the compliance burden or penalty regime becomes too harsh.
