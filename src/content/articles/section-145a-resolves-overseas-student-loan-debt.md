In an opinion piece for interest.co.nz, tax specialist Dave Ananth sets out how section 145A of the Student Loan Scheme Act 2011 offers Inland Revenue a practical mechanism for dealing with student loan debts that have ballooned over time for borrowers who are now based overseas.

Ananth points to IRD figures showing that 73,732 file referrals were made to overseas debt collection agencies between the 2022/23 and 2025/26 financial years. He notes that while this level of enforcement activity puts pressure on offshore borrowers, traditional recovery methods often produce limited returns when the underlying debt is decades old and has accumulated significant interest.

According to Ananth, section 145A, read together with related statutory provisions, gives the Commissioner of Inland Revenue discretionary power to write off equitable amounts of ordinary loan interest and to cancel accrued interest in appropriate cases. This discretion becomes available once a borrower re-engages with IRD, makes a full and honest disclosure of their financial position, and puts forward a realistic proposal for repayment, whether as a lump sum or through instalments.

Ananth is careful to stress that this is not an automatic discount or a blanket amnesty for overseas borrowers. Relief under section 145A depends on the borrower coming forward, being transparent about their circumstances, and committing to a genuine settlement rather than simply waiting out enforcement action.

Drawing on his own practical experience advising clients in this area, Ananth explains that when borrowers do engage properly and provide full financial disclosure, the outcome can work well for both sides. The Crown is often able to recover meaningful sums that might otherwise have remained uncollectable, while the borrower gets the chance to permanently resolve historical debt that has been growing due to accrued interest.

For New Zealanders overseas carrying old, escalating student loan balances, Ananth's analysis suggests that proactive engagement with IRD, rather than avoidance, may be the more effective path towards a lasting resolution under the current legislative framework.
