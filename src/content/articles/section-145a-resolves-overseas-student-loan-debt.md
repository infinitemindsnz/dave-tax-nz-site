In an opinion piece for Interest.co.nz, tax specialist Dave Ananth explains how section 145A of the Student Loan Scheme Act 2011 offers a practical route for Inland Revenue (IRD) to resolve long-standing, ballooned student loan debts owed by borrowers who are now living overseas.

Ananth notes that between the 2022/23 and 2025/26 financial years, IRD made 73,732 file referrals to overseas debt collectors. He observes that while this level of enforcement activity creates pressure on borrowers, traditional recovery methods often produce limited returns when debts have accumulated over decades.

According to Ananth, section 145A, read alongside related statutory provisions, gives the Commissioner discretionary authority to write off equitable amounts of ordinary loan interest and cancel accrued interest. This discretion becomes available once a borrower re-engages with IRD, provides full financial disclosure, and puts forward a realistic settlement proposal, whether as a lump sum or through instalments.

He is careful to stress that this mechanism is not an automatic discount or a blanket amnesty. Rather, it depends on the borrower coming forward voluntarily and demonstrating genuine financial circumstances.

Drawing on his own practical experience, Ananth explains that when borrowers are transparent about their financial position and present a serious, workable proposal, the Crown is often able to recover meaningful sums that might otherwise have remained uncollectable. At the same time, borrowers gain the benefit of finally and permanently resolving debt that may have followed them for years while living abroad.

The piece frames section 145A as a pragmatic middle ground: a way to balance IRD's enforcement obligations against the practical reality that pursuing overseas-based borrowers for old, growing debts does not always deliver good outcomes for either side.
