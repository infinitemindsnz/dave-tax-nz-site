On 30 August 2026, Three News broadcast a segment examining the National Party's newly announced student loan policy, branded the "Back Pocket Boost for Graduates." According to the broadcast, the policy would introduce structural changes to the Student Loan Scheme if National is re-elected, built around a two-tiered approach: incentives designed to encourage graduates to remain in New Zealand, paired with a stricter stance on borrowers who default while living overseas.

The segment, which ran between the 0:43 and 4:09 marks of the bulletin, included commentary from Dave Ananth, a tax barrister and specialist in overseas student loan debt at Meridian Partners. As reported by Three News, Ananth acknowledged that student loan debt is funded by taxpayers and ultimately needs to be repaid. At the same time, he cautioned that increasing penalties on overseas-based borrowers could prove counterproductive, warning that harsher measures risk pushing already disengaged borrowers further away from re-engaging with their obligations rather than encouraging repayment.

The Three News report frames the announcement as part of National's broader election-year positioning on student debt, contrasting encouragement for graduates who stay in New Zealand with a firmer line on those who leave and fall behind on repayments while overseas. Ananth's industry perspective, as featured in the segment, adds a practitioner's caution to the policy debate: that enforcement-driven approaches to overseas default need to be weighed against the practical effect they may have on voluntary compliance.

Further detail on the specific mechanics of the proposed reform, including how incentives and penalties would be structured, was not covered in the portion of the broadcast summarised here.
